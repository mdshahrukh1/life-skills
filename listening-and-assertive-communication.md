# Listening and Active Communication

## 1. What are the steps/strategies for Active Listening?
   
* Active Listening: 6 quick strategies mentioned below-

* Put the phone away. Give the person your full attention.

* Show you’re with them. Nod, keep eye contact, don’t look bored.

* Don’t interrupt. Let them finish before you react.

* Notice tone and body language. Words aren’t the whole story.

* Use small prompts. “Okay,” “I see,” “Tell me more.”

* Ask a clarifying question. Make sure you actually got it right.


## 2. According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening? 

* Fisher’s model in simple words.
  
* Focus fully — no distractions, no half-listening
  
* Try to see it their way — understand, even if you don’t agree.
  
* Match the mood — notice their emotions and reflect them back gently.
  
* Summarise in your own words — show you understood, not just heard.
  
* Respond to the point — don’t drift into your own unrelated story.
  

## 3. What are the obstacles in your listening process?

* Obstacles in my listening are that my mind wanders and I start planning what I’ll say next.
  
* I start thinking of different scenarios.
  
* I create my own thoughts around it.

* I sometimes judge or assume too early.

* Phone notifications and background noise pull me out.
  

## 4. What can you do to improve your listening?

* I can improve my listening by abiding by these rules
  
* Pause before replying.
  
* When I am listening, I have to stop my mind from thinking and focus on only listening.
  
* Take a few notes in important conversations.
  
* Ask “Can you say more about that?” instead of assuming.
  

## 5. When do you switch to a passive communication style in your day-to-day life?

* I switch to a passive communication style in my day-to-day life when I am talking to senior people, when I’m nervous about disagreeing.

* When I want to avoid an argument, I just go along with things.


## 6. When do you switch into Aggressive communication styles in your day-to-day life?

* I switch into aggressive communication styles in my day-to-day life when I’m stressed and feel others aren’t doing their part.

* When I feel disrespected and react before thinking.
  

## 7. When do you switch into passive-aggressive (sarcasm/gossiping/taunts/silent treatment and others) communication styles in your day-to-day life?

* I go passive-aggressive with close friends/family when I don’t want a big fight — sarcasm or silent treatment.

* When I feel powerless, I drop hints instead of saying what’s wrong.
  

## 8. How can you make your communication assertive? 

* Here are a few points on how I can make my communication assertive

* Using “I” statements — “I feel…” instead of “You always…”.

* Say it clearly and calmly — honest, direct, not harsh.

* Watch body language — steady voice, relaxed posture, normal eye contact.

* Practice hard conversations — rehearse or write down key points.
  
* Learn to say no — without guilt or a long excuse.
